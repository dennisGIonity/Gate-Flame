Found it. It's spread across three places, all in the **Gate^Flame** work — not one single chat.

**1. Where I laid out inline as an option — session "Mobile connection issues diagnosis", 18 Aug 2026**

In the "Takeover" question I gave you four options. Inline was option 4:

> **Inline (box sits between router and LAN)** — Two NICs, all traffic physically passes through the box. Total and unambiguous control of DNS, IPv4 and IPv6, with no spoofing and no router config. But the customer has to re-cable, the box becomes a single point of failure for the whole house, and it forces a hardware/BOM change — the Orange Pi Zero 2W base model has one NIC.

That's the "protects each device" argument: if every packet physically crosses the box, there's nothing to spoof, nothing to opt out, and every client is individually visible.

**2. Why the router-only path can't do that — ADR-001, same session, 24 Aug 2026**

The commit message spells out the cost of the router-forwarding design you chose:

> Filtering is not 100% — router resolvers learn their fastest upstream and fall back only on failure, so the leak is small but real. **Per-client attribution is lost: Pi-hole sees the router, not each device**, so nothing on the standard box can promise per-device history and kiosk copy must not imply total coverage.

And the reason you took that trade anyway (load shedding):

> Any design that points devices at this box turns a power cut into a whole-house outage with no automatic recovery… As an upstream, the same power cut costs the household its filtering and nothing else.

**3. The doc version — `gateflame-two-tier-endgame.md` §1.1**

Same two costs, named as accepted: *"a small DNS leak rate… and per-client attribution lost in Pi-hole under router-forwarding mode."* Also A7: *"No claim = no traffic crossing the box = nothing to bounce"* — firewall bounce and DPI ship dead without traffic actually traversing the device.
